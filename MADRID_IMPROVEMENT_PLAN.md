# Where and how Madrid can improve: lessons from five benchmark cities

*IE Sustainability Datathon 2026 · Kearney: Zero-Emission Urban Bus Fleets · 20 Nov 2026*
*Based on `ALL_CITIES_BENCHMARK.md` (Brussels, London, Berlin, Helsinki, Paris) and the Madrid model (`Madrid/outputs/File_2–4`).*

## Bottom line

**S1 (100% zero-emission by 31 Dec 2030) is not realistic, and no peer has done it.** Madrid's own model shows why: S1 needs **+444, +410, +424 and +390** buses in 2027–2030 (`File_4`), about **3.5× EMT's best year ever (116 in 2024)** and **~€300 M capex a year** vs ~€72 M in S0. Two of five depots (Sanchinarro, Entrevías) are **Critical** on grid. Every peer that aimed at 2030 moved: Brussels → 2035, London → 2034 without extra funding, Berlin and Helsinki → 2035, Paris has no zero-emission target.

**Our proposal: an infrastructure-paced transition** (each depot electrified when its grid is in place, CNG retired at ~12 years). At ~250 e-buses/yr, the remaining ~1,650 CNG buses are gone by **~2033**; at today's ~165/yr pace, **~2036**.

## 2. Ten improvements, each tied to a peer number

### A. Grid and depots (where the transition is decided)

**1. Request grid capacity years before the buses: start with Sanchinarro phase 2.**
- *Peers:* London waits **3–10 years** for ~10 MVA; Berlin's grid operator could allocate only **365 of ~2,300 MVA** requested in 2025.
- *Madrid:* Sanchinarro needs **17.4 MW** (managed, `File_3`) vs **8 MW** installed, about **9–11 MW short**. Entrevías needs 13.1 MW vs an estimated 8.7 MW. File both requests now and phase every depot's connection 5–10 years ahead.
- *Watch:* MW requested vs MW granted per depot, per year.

**2. Add capacity through new or split depots, not just bigger ones.**
- *Peers:* Berlin's two new e-only depots (Säntisstraße, Treptow, 2027) turn **1 Critical / 5 Upgrade into 0 Critical / 4 Upgrade**. London's median garage has **~91 buses**; Paris averages **~185**.
- *Madrid:* depots of ~400 buses each need 10–17 MW. Treat La Elipa (≤20 MW, ~2029) as Madrid's "Säntisstraße": protect its date, because until it opens Fuencarral carries **764 buses (~29 MW if electric)** and +3,700 empty km per weekday. Add satellite overnight sites of 50–150 buses near outer terminals.
- *Watch:* largest depot's MW need; empty km per weekday.

**3. Make managed charging, and fewer chargers than buses, the design standard.**
- *Peers:* Paris runs smart charging in 6 depots, **605 chargers for 745 buses, −10% charging cost**; Berlin IGS has **0.48** chargers per e-bus; London Holloway **40 for 57**.
- *Madrid:* the model needs **71 MW managed vs 204 MW unmanaged** across five depots. Managed charging is what makes the grid figures workable at all.
- *Watch:* MW peak per depot vs connection; chargers per e-bus.

**4. Pilot battery storage at Sanchinarro.**
- *Peer:* London Southall runs **25 e-buses on 500 kVA + a 1.2 MWh battery**.
- *Madrid:* a battery at Sanchinarro stretches the 8 MW connection while phase 2 is pending. Consider energy-as-a-service (London's Zenobē model) to keep the asset off EMT's balance sheet.
- *Watch:* e-buses charged per MW of connection.

### B. Charging strategy

**5. Use terminal charging only for the six long Sanchinarro lines, and file those grid requests first.**
- *Peers:* Berlin line 200 has charged at terminals since 2020 (**2 × 450 kW at each end**) and BVG is adding **up to 20 terminal chargers by 2027**; Helsinki keeps it to **8 long routes**; London route 358's terminal charger **slipped 18 months**.
- *Madrid:* lines 203, 200, 164, 179, SE709 and 88 exceed a depot battery. With **91% of buses out at noon**, terminal top-ups are Madrid's only daytime charging option.
- *Watch:* lines over battery limit; terminal grid requests filed.

**6. Test whether a midday charging window can be created.**
- *Peers:* Brussels (**64%** out at noon) and Helsinki (**67%**, "charging during daytime breaks") spread the load over two windows; Madrid runs 91%.
- *Madrid:* test midday bus swaps on lines with a weaker off-peak demand before buying bigger batteries.
- *Watch:* % of peak buses at 12:00 per depot.

### C. Pace, procurement and money

**7. Commit pace through multi-year contracts tied to depot readiness, not a yearly budget.**
- *Peers:* Helsinki writes a **minimum electric share of route-km into every tender** and went **10 → 600 e-buses (52%)** in seven years. London's electric share of new contracts rose **27% → 60%** (2021–26). Berlin depended on a yearly budget: when it was cut from **€33.1 M to €8 M (2024)**, additions fell to **+1**.
- *Madrid:* a 2026–2035 fleet plan of **~250 e-buses a year**, with each order released when its depot's grid is ready, and a published line-by-line calendar per depot.
- *Watch:* e-buses added per year vs plan; orders vs depots ready.

**8. No new combustion buses; treat biomethane as a stopgap, not a target.**
- *Peers:* Paris runs **1,300 biomethane + 1,100 hybrid** buses and so has **no zero-emission target**; Brussels' ~400 hybrids count as diesel under the LEZ; London stopped buying hybrids in 2021.
- *Madrid:* EMT bought CNG until 2023, so at end 2030 **984 of the 1,687 CNG buses would be under 12 years old**. Retire them near end of life rather than writing them off; biomethane in the remaining CNG fleet can cut emissions meanwhile but must not be counted as zero-emission.
- *Watch:* CNG buses retired before 12 years (target: zero).

**9. Blend funding sources like Berlin.**
- *Peer:* Berlin's 320 articulated e-buses are funded **~50% BVG / ~40% federal / ~10% Land**, plus **€12.1 M federal** for terminal chargers.
- *Madrid:* the only EU money in our data is **€4.69 M CEF** for Sanchinarro (of €12.58 M). The grid and depot bill is larger than buses: Berlin's depots alone cost ~€1.3 bn. Apply for CEF-AFIF and EIB loans depot by depot.
- *Watch:* € external funding per depot.

### D. Transparency

**10. Publish depot-by-depot grid and conversion data.**
- *Peer:* Berlin's Senate publishes each depot's conversion year (Spandau 2030, Lichtenberg 2033, Müllerstraße 2035) and its grid-allocation report.
- *Madrid:* grid figures for Carabanchel, Fuencarral and Entrevías (**1,331 buses, ~60% of the fleet**) are not published. A public depot calendar makes the plan auditable and the grid requests credible.
- *Watch:* depots with a published MW and conversion year (target: 5 of 5).

## 3. Proposed timeline (infrastructure-paced, our counter-scenario to S1)

| Phase | Years | What happens | Peer precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Secure the grid | 2026–2027 | File Sanchinarro phase 2 (~9–11 MW), Entrevías upgrade, terminal requests for the 6 long lines; managed charging in all depots; battery pilot at Sanchinarro | Berlin's grid rationing; London Southall |
| 2. Rebuild and split | 2027–2029 | La Elipa reopens (≤20 MW, 318 pantographs); Fuencarral relieved; first satellite site | Berlin Säntisstraße/Treptow 2027 |
| 3. Scale | 2029–2033 | ~250 e-buses/yr, each order tied to a ready depot; CNG retired at ~12 years | Helsinki ~85/yr on a fleet half Madrid's size |
| 4. Finish | 2033–2035 | Last CNG out (~2033 at 250/yr; ~2036 at 165/yr) | Brussels 2035, Helsinki 2035, London 2034 |

Still model S1 year by year as the brief requires, to show where it breaks: first at the Sanchinarro and Entrevías grid, then at ~€300 M/yr capex.

## 4. Pitch lines

- "Every city we benchmarked moved its 2030 goal, and the reason was always depots and grid, never the buses."
- "Madrid's buses can do the distance; its depots can't take the power yet: Sanchinarro has 8 MW and needs 17."
- "Helsinki went from 10 to 600 e-buses by writing electrification into every contract. Berlin's pace collapsed to one bus when its budget was cut. Pace is a governance choice."
- "Paris looks clean, but 70% of its target mix is biomethane. Madrid should not repeat that with CNG."
