02 · THE DEPOT BOTTLENECK

The real problem is not the bus. It is the power.

If all 2,042 buses needed at peak were battery-electric, EMT's five depots would have to put 447 MWh into batteries every night. Spread over a 7-hour charging window with smart (managed) charging, that is 71.0 MW. The five depots' combined connection is an estimated 57.3 MW.

Each column: the filled block is the grid connection (solid = Evidenced, hatched = Estimated proxy, never a confirmed utility offer); the dark bar is the managed overnight charging power needed if the depot's assigned lines ran 100 % battery-electric; red = shortfall. Managed MW = night energy ÷ (window × 0.90 efficiency). Source: File 3; sensitivity per report §3.

23.3
MW short across the network

An electric bus can be purchased and delivered relatively quickly. A substation, grid connection or major depot upgrade can take years.

That is why infrastructure, not vehicle supply, sets the speed of fleet electrification. London reports 3–10 years from grid application to energisation; Brussels' Marly II depot took about four years.

Sanchinarro and Entrevías are the most constrained depots. Sanchinarro serves the longest lines from an 8 MW supply and would need 2.17× its connection. Entrevías' connection is not published and is an estimate. The rebuilt La Elipa, with a 20 MW planned ceiling, is the only depot with real headroom, but it reopens only in 2029. No plausible connection hypothesis tested in the report makes Sanchinarro Ready.

03 · THE NETWORK AND ITS LINES

Not all bus lines are equally easy to electrify.

We evaluated all 234 EMT lines running on Tuesday 17 November 2026. 228 can run all day on overnight depot charging. Six cannot: their buses cover more kilometres a day than a standard battery bus is assumed to manage on one charge (300 km). They need opportunity charging at a terminal.

    Sequencing matters. Starting with high-impact, depot-feasible lines (19, 27, C1, C2…) delivers CO₂ and street-level benefits early while the organisation learns. The six long Sanchinarro lines come last and get terminal chargers. Terminal charging removes only 0.8 % of depot energy: it is a range fix, not a grid fix.

    05 · THE RECOMMENDED ROADMAP Supplementary · not in Files 1–4

    A transition that can actually be delivered.

    S3 lets the CNG fleet's own age set the pace. No new CNG bus is bought from 2026. Intake rises to about 150 e-buses a year from 2029, when La Elipa reopens, and every CNG bus retires near the end of its life. The last cohort, bought in 2023, leaves in 2038.

    2030
    Depot readiness this year: no action (grey) vs with mitigations (colour) · dashed = connection limit
    The strategy
    1. Stop buying new CNG buses from 2026.
    2. Procure about 150 e-buses a year from 2029 through multi-OEM frameworks.
    3. Replace CNG buses in line with their lifecycle, at about 13 years on average, as EMT does today.
    4. File grid applications in 2027, sized for the 2038 fleet, so upgrades are live by 2032.
    5. Use smart charging everywhere, plus targeted batteries and terminal chargers at Sanchinarro.
    6. Avoid premature retirement: CNG is the buffer if infrastructure slips.
    Charging mitigations
    • 2026–27Smart charging at all depots; grid applications for all four live depots
    • 2028Fuencarral pinch (0.94×): cap charge power, hold deliveries
    • 2029La Elipa reopens: Fuencarral falls to 0.69×
    • 2031Sanchinarro: terminal chargers (≈15 % of energy) + day-charged battery (≈1 MW)
    • 2032–34+5 MW each at Carabanchel, Fuencarral and Entrevías; Sanchinarro +4 MW, then +4 MW
    • BufferLa Elipa keeps ≈9.6 MW headroom; if late, slow intake and keep CNG longer

    Mitigation sizes are planning assumptions (register A37–A39), to be confirmed with EMT and the distributors.

    The trade-off, in one picture

    CO₂ avoided over 2026–35 against the highest single year of spending. Bubble size = stranded CNG value.

    S3 avoids about 425 kt of CO₂ over 2026–35, against 623 kt for S2 and 800 kt for S1. In exchange, it cuts peak annual capex to about €145 M, cuts stranded value to about €62 M, and gives the grid a six-year runway: no depot needs new capacity before 2033. From 2038, it avoids about 107 kt a year.

    Procurement · depots

    London

    Every new bus has been zero-emission since 2021. Garages with weak connections use smart charging and on-site batteries (Southall: 500 kVA + 1.2 MWh).

    For Madrid: ZE-only purchases from 2026; a day-charged battery at Sanchinarro.

    Limit: TfL tenders routes to private operators who own the buses; EMT carries the capex itself.
    E-native depots · terminals

    Berlin

    BVG is building two ~200-bus electric depots and a small network of terminal fast chargers (line 200: 2 × 450 kW).

    For Madrid: design Las Tablas as an e-native relief depot; terminal chargers only for the six long Sanchinarro lines.

    Limit: Berlin rations depot grid capacity; there is no firm 100 % zero-tailpipe year.
    Scheduling · phasing

    Brussels

    STIB's published vehicle blocks show range is rarely binding when schedules are designed for it. STIB has moved its target from 2030 to end-2035.

    For Madrid: reschedule long blocks before buying range; plan for multi-year depot projects.

    Limit: a much smaller bus network (51 lines) with trams and metro carrying most demand.
    Tenders · clean grid

    Helsinki

    52 % zero-emission, with ZE criteria in operator tenders and a 57.5 g/kWh grid.

    For Madrid: put ZE minimums in every procurement. At 108.4 g/kWh, each e-bus already cuts CO₂ by ≈87 % vs CNG.

    Limit: HSL tenders operations (operators bring buses and depots); the fleet figure is a proxy.
    07 · THE DECISIONS THAT MATTER

    The transition is possible. But the conditions must be right.

    Four conditions decide whether Madrid reaches 100 % zero-emission in 2038, or faster. If all four are secured early and co-funding is added, the same plan accelerates towards 2033.

    01

    Grid filed in 2027, live by 2032

    Applications for the four constrained depots, sized for 2038 loads. Fuencarral and Entrevías capacities are proxies: the first action is to obtain firm figures.

    02

    ~150 e-buses a year

    Multi-OEM procurement frameworks: 1.3× EMT's record year, a pace suppliers and workshops can plan for.

    03

    La Elipa open by 2029

    20 MW and 318 places: the only depot with spare capacity, about 9.6 MW even in 2038.

    04

    €110–145 M a year

    Stable funding through 2038. Peak year €145 M (2036), against €340 M under S1.

    § · METHODOLOGY AND SOURCES

    How we know what we know.

    Definitions and reference dates. Fleet baseline: 2,210 buses at 31 Dec 2025 (452 BEV + 10 H₂ + 1,748 CNG; 20.9 % ZE). Lines and kilometres: EMT GTFS (feed 6 Nov 2026), reference day Tuesday 17 Nov 2026: 234 lines, 339,220 in-service km. Peak buses: EMT supply file, proxy day Tue 12 May 2026 (2,042 buses).

    Energy and power. 1.30 / 1.85 kWh/km (standard / articulated), ranges 300 / 240 km, 100 kW chargers, 7-hour window, 90 % efficiency (brief §4.5). Managed MW = night energy ÷ (7 × 0.90).

    Depots. Lines assigned from EMT's historical-lines table. Connections: Carabanchel 10 MVA, Sanchinarro 8 MW and La Elipa 20 MW (planned ceiling) are Evidenced; Fuencarral and Entrevías are Estimated from Carabanchel's MW per bus. Readiness is indicative, not a utility approval.

    Scenarios. S0 and S1 are File 4 (2026–2035). S2 and S3 are supplementary scenarios run on the same notebook engine; S3 is extended to 2038. They are modelled paths, not commitments. Capex in constant 2025 €, ex-VAT, undiscounted. CO₂ = CNG tank-to-wheel (MITECO) + electricity generation (REE 2025, 108.4 g/kWh, held constant).
